Part 01The one-minute version

01The one-minute version

If you only memorise one paragraph, memorise this one.

"I build payroll and tax software for South African businesses, and every number in it comes from the SARS tables rather than from what I thought the rule was."

That sentence works because it says three things at once: the domain (payroll and tax in South Africa), the differentiator (the numbers are right, and I can prove it), and the attitude (I check the source instead of guessing).

Then, if they want more, there are three directions you can go, and you should pick the one that fits the person you are talking to:

To a recruiter

Lead with the payroll suite. It is the biggest thing, it has a database, an API and a dashboard, and it solves a problem a real business has. Mention that it has 148 tests and that the tax figures are checked against SARS's own published tables.

To an accountant

Lead with the domain knowledge. The annualised method, the s11F cap, the year-end reconciliation, the EMP201 against the IRP5. This is where a BCom student has an unfair advantage and should use it.

To a developer

Lead with the engineering. Zero dependencies, the engine separated from the database, a JSON API with no framework, and the same algorithm in Python and JavaScript with shared tests.

Part 02What was built

02What was built

Four projects, built in order. Each one needed the one before it, so they read as a single body of work rather than four separate assignments.

Why there are four, and not one

Each project exists because the previous one ran into a wall.

  • The calculator asked "what does one person pay?" It needed the SARS tables, and it needed them to be right — so it needed tests with hand-worked answers.
  • The employer tool asked "what does a business owe?" That brought in VAT, SDL, the EMP201 and a batch of employees, which a single payslip calculator could not hold.
  • The payroll suite asked "what happens when it is a real business?" That needed a database, history, forms that reconcile and a dashboard somebody could actually look at.
  • The site asked "how does anyone find out?" It needed the models to run in a browser, which meant porting the engine to JavaScript — and the port is what proved the engine was clean.
Say this if asked "why four projects instead of one big one?"
Because each one was a real answer to a real problem, and a person can read and judge one project properly. Four small, finished, tested things are more convincing than one large, unfinished thing.

Part 03The accounting

03The accounting you need

The slabs: tax is progressive, not flat

South African income tax is progressive. Each band has its own rate, and you only pay that rate on the part of your income that falls inside it. That is why the tax table is written as "R44 118 plus 26% of the amount over R245 100" and not "26% of everything".

The worked example to have ready. A salary of R30 000 a month:

1. Annualised taxable income = R30 000 × 12 = R360 000
2. It falls in the second band, so tax = R44 118 + 26% × (R360 000 − R245 100) = R44 118 + R29 874 = R73 992
3. Less the primary rebate of R17 820 = R56 172 a year
4. Monthly PAYE = R56 172 ÷ 12 = R4 681
5. Plus UIF of 1% capped at R17 712 = R177.12
6. Net pay = R30 000 − R4 681 − R177.12 = R25 141.88

The annualised method: the rule people get wrong

Because the brackets are built around a year of income, you cannot tax a month on its own. The month's taxable income is annualised, taxed, and divided by twelve again.

The rules that catch people out

RuleWhat it actually saysWhy it matters
Rebates Primary R17 820 for everyone. Add R9 765 at 65, and a further R3 249 at 75. Rebates are a credit against the tax, not an exemption from income. They are why someone on R99 000 pays nothing at all.
Medical tax credit R376 a month for the member, R376 for the first dependant, R254 for each one after (2027). Also a credit against the tax, and you keep the higher of the table amount and what you actually pay.
Section 11F A pension deduction is capped at the lowest of the actual contribution, 27.5% of remuneration, and R350 000 a year. Without the cap, a high earner could shelter most of their income and pay no tax at all.
UIF 1% from the employee, 1% from the employer, on remuneration up to R17 712 a month. So the maximum anyone ever contributes is R177.12 a month, and commission earners with half their pay in commission are excluded.
SDL 1% of the leviable amount, and employers with an annual payroll below R500 000 pay none. A business cost, not a deduction from the employee. It is the employer's own money.
VAT 15% on supplies, 15% claimed back on inputs. Mandatory registration above R2.3m a year, voluntary above R120 000 (both from 1 April 2026). Output tax less input tax. If the input is bigger, you claim the difference back.
Interest The first R23 800 a year is exempt (R34 500 at 65 and over). The exemption is used up first; only what is above it is taxable.
Dividends Exempt from income tax, but 20% is withheld at source as a final tax. You are done with it — there is nothing more to pay on a South African dividend.

The three forms an employer files

MONTHLY

EMP201

The monthly employer declaration. It declares the total payment and splits it between PAYE, UIF and SDL. Due by the 7th of the month after the month it covers — and if the 7th is a Saturday, the Friday before.

YEARLY

EMP501

The annual employer reconciliation. It proves that what was declared on the twelve EMP201s is the same as what the IRP5 certificates say was paid to the employees. Any difference has to be explained.

PER EMPLOYEE

IRP5

The employee's tax certificate for the year: what they were paid, what was deducted, and what the tax came to. Issued within 60 days of the end of the tax year.

The best thing to say about the deadline logic: a deadline that falls on a public holiday is not on the public holiday. My code rolls every date back to the previous business day, and there is a test that checks an October payroll is due on Friday 6 November 2026, because the 7th is a Saturday. Small detail, but it is the kind of detail that separates a real payroll from a toy one.

Part 05The accounting concepts

05The concepts, one by one

Start with the seven that only a finance student would have.

A1

Progressive slab rates

What it is
Tax is charged band by band. Each band has its own rate, and the rate applies only to the slice of income inside it. The SARS table is written as "R44 118 plus 26% of the amount over R245 100".
Where it lives
tax_tables.py → TAX_YEARS["2027"]["brackets"], and the arithmetic in engine.py → annualised_tax().
Why it matters
Getting this wrong means every number downstream is wrong. It is the foundation everything else sits on.

Say it like this: "South African tax is progressive, so I don't apply one rate to the whole salary — I find the band the annualised income falls in and apply that rate only to the part above the band's floor."

A2

The annualised method

What it is
Because the brackets are built around a year of income, a month cannot be taxed alone. The month's taxable income is multiplied by twelve, taxed, and divided by twelve again.
Where it lives
engine.py → annualised_tax(), and the same idea in portfolio/assets/js/tax.js.
Why it matters
It smooths the tax: a December bonus does not push somebody into a higher bracket for the whole year, and a month with no overtime does not drop them into a lower one. It also creates the mid-year-joiner problem that the year-end reconciliation exists to fix.

Say it like this: "I annualise the month's taxable income, tax that, then divide by twelve to get the PAYE. It means bonuses get spread rather than hitting one hard month."

A3

Rebates and tax thresholds

What it is
A rebate is a credit against the tax, not an exemption from income. Primary R17 820 for everyone, plus R9 765 at 65 and R3 249 at 75. The threshold is where the rebate exactly cancels the tax — R99 000 for someone under 65 in 2027.
Where it lives
tax_tables.py → rebates, applied in engine.py → annualised_tax().
Why it matters
It is why somebody earning R99 000 pays no tax at all, and why the secondary rebate only exists for pensioners. Treat rebates as an exemption and you will be wrong by R17 820 for most people.

Say it like this: "A rebate comes off the tax after the slab rates, not off the income before them — that difference is worth R17 820 to almost everybody."

A4

The medical scheme fees tax credit

What it is
Also a credit against the tax. R376 a month for the member, R376 for the first dependant and R254 for each dependant after that, for 2027. You keep the higher of the table amount and what you actually pay for your own membership.
Where it lives
tax_tables.py → medical_credit, and medical_credit() in both engines.
Why it matters
It is a real amount of money — R18 912 a year for a family — and it is the thing most people leave on the table. It also has to be applied after the rebates, not before.

Say it like this: "The medical credit is a rebate too, and you take the higher of the tabulated amount and what you actually pay — so a member on an expensive scheme is not penalised."

A5

Section 11F: the pension cap

What it is
A pension or provident fund contribution is deductible, but only up to the lowest of three limits: the actual contribution, 27.5% of remuneration, and R350 000 a year.
Where it lives
payroll.py → allowance_for_retirement() and engine.py → calculate_pension().
Why it matters
Without the cap a high earner could put most of their salary into a pension and pay no tax at all. The cap is the anti-avoidance rule, and it is the kind of thing that makes the tax calculation real rather than arithmetic.

Say it like this: "The pension deduction is capped at 27.5% of pay and R350 000 a year, whichever is smaller — that cap is what stops the deduction from becoming a tax-avoidance scheme."

A6

UIF and the earnings ceiling

What it is
One percent from the employee and one percent from the employer, on remuneration, but only up to R17 712 a month. So nobody ever contributes more than R177.12 a month, whatever they earn. Commission earners whose commission is half or more of their pay are excluded entirely.
Where it lives
uif.py and engine.py → calculateUIF().
Why it matters
It is the clearest example in the project of a rule that a naive implementation gets wrong — applying 1% to the whole salary is the obvious mistake.

Say it like this: "UIF is one percent each side but only on the first R17 712 of pay, so it caps out at R177.12 a month — and commission earners are excluded."

A7

SDL and the small-employer exemption

What it is
One percent of the leviable amount — remuneration after the allowable deductions — and employers with an annual payroll below R500 000 pay none of it. It is a business cost; you may not take it off the employee.
Where it lives
payroll.py → calculate_sdl(), with the exemption at config.py → SDL_ANNUAL_PAYROLL_EXEMPTION.
Why it matters
It is the difference between a small business's cost base and a large one's, and it is decided by the employer's annual payroll, not the employee's. Getting that scope wrong is a classic bug.

Say it like this: "SDL is a percent of the leviable amount and it's the employer's cost, and below R500 000 of annual payroll the employer is exempt entirely."

Part 05The employer and the forms

05The employer's side

B1

Output tax, input tax, and the net

What it is
Output tax is 15% of what you charged. Input tax is 15% of what you can claim back. The difference is what you pay SARS; if the input is bigger, you claim the difference back.
Where it lives
vat.py → calculate_vat() and vat.js → calculateVat().
Why it matters
It is simpler than it looks and easier to get wrong in the other direction — a business that over-claimed input tax is committing an offence.

Say it like this: "Output tax less input tax is the net position, and a negative net is a refund rather than a payment."

B2

The six bi-monthly VAT periods

What it is
A tax year runs 1 March to the end of February, and a vendor files a VAT201 every two months: March–April, May–June, July–August, September–October, November–December, January–February. Businesses with taxable supplies over R30 million file monthly instead.
Where it lives
vat.py → vat_periods(), mirrored in vat.js.
Why it matters
Each period has a different deadline, and the deadline is in the month after the period ends — so the March–April period is due in May, not in April.

Say it like this: "Six two-month periods, and the return for a period is due in the month after that period ends — the March–April one is due in May."

B3

The EMP201, and the seventh of the month

What it is
The monthly declaration: the total payment, split between PAYE, UIF and SDL. Due within seven days after the month ends, so by the 7th of the following month.
Where it lives
compliance.py → emp201() and emp201_due_date().
Why it matters
Late payment carries a 10% penalty plus daily interest, and the declaration is the employer's own liability — if you get it wrong, the money is still owed.

Say it like this: "The EMP201 declares the total and splits it three ways, and it's due by the seventh of the next month — or the Friday before, if the seventh is a weekend."

B4

The EMP501 reconciliation

What it is
Once a year, the employer proves that the twelve EMP201 declarations add up to what the IRP5 certificates say was actually paid to the employees. Any difference has to be explained.
Where it lives
compliance.py → emp501(), and the same check is in tests/test_compliance.py.
Why it matters
It is the one place where the employer's tax year closes. A payroll that produces payslips but cannot reconcile them is not finished.

Say it like this: "The EMP501 reconciles the twelve monthly declarations against the tax certificates, so the number the employer declared and the number the employee was taxed on are proven to be the same."

Part 05The engineering concepts

05The engineering concepts

E1

The standard library only

What it is
No pip install, no requirements.txt, no virtual environment. sqlite3, http.server, json, csv, argparse, unittest and datetime do all of it.
Where it lives
Every Python file. The pyproject.toml lists dependencies = [].
Why it matters
It means the project runs on any machine with Python, forever, with nothing to break. It is also a discipline: you learn the tool instead of the wrapper around it.

Say it like this: "I used nothing but the standard library, so the whole thing runs on any machine with Python and there is no install step and nothing to go stale."

E2

Separation of concerns

What it is
The tax rules, the storage and the presentation are three separate layers, and they only point one way. The engine does not know a database exists; the CLI does not know how the tax is calculated.
Where it lives
engine.py (rules), db.py (storage), cli.py and server.py (presentation).
Why it matters
It is why 58 of the 148 checks can test the tax calculation on its own, with no database, in milliseconds. Layered code is not slower to write — it is faster to change.

Say it like this: "The engine never touches a database or the terminal, so the tax can be tested in isolation, and the same engine serves both the CLI and the API."

E3

Dataclasses as the domain model

What it is
Every real thing in the domain — an employee, a pay run, a payslip — is a dataclass with named fields, plus a to_row() and from_row() pair so it can go into and come out of SQLite without an ORM.
Where it lives
payroll_suite/models.py.
Why it matters
The names in the code are the names in the accounting. medical_dependants means the same thing in the code, in the database and in the conversation with an employer.

Say it like this: "I modelled the domain as dataclasses with a row mapping, so the code reads like the accounting and there is no ORM in the way."

E4

SQLite, foreign keys and schema stamping

What it is
A single file database. Foreign keys are switched on, the schema is created with IF NOT EXISTS, and a meta table stamps the schema version so an old database keeps working when the code moves on.
Where it lives
payroll_suite/db.py.
Why it matters
One file means the whole business's payroll can be copied onto a USB stick, backed up by copying it, and shipped to a client as one attachment.

Say it like this: "The whole payroll is one SQLite file, so backup is copying it, and the schema stamps its version so old data never breaks."

E5

The WSGI adapter

What it is
Most free Python hosts want a WSGI callable, not a script. The suite ships wsgi.py, which wraps the same API in a WSGI application, so the identical code runs on a laptop and on a free host.
Where it lives
payroll_suite/wsgi.py.
Why it matters
It means "run it locally" and "host it for free" are the same code, not two implementations that drift apart.

Say it like this: "I wrote a small WSGI adapter so the same code runs on a free host as well as locally, rather than maintaining two servers."

E6

A JSON API with real error handling

What it is
Eight endpoints, a router, a typed error class, and errors that come back as JSON with a message somebody can act on. The dashboard only ever talks to it in JSON.
Where it lives
payroll_suite/server.py, and the same routes again in wsgi.py.
Why it matters
The API is what makes the dashboard possible without a framework, and it means the calculations can be driven by anything that can send JSON.

Say it like this: "Eight JSON endpoints, no framework, and the errors come back as readable messages rather than stack traces."

E7

Test fixtures worked out by hand

What it is
Every expected number in the test suite was calculated by hand from the SARS tables and typed in as a literal. No test computes its own expectation with the same code it is testing.
Where it lives
tests/test_engine.py, tests/test_vat.py, tests/test_payroll.py and tests/test_compliance.py.
Why it matters
It is the difference between testing and checking that a function still does what it did. A test that recomputes the answer proves nothing.

Say it like this: "Every expected figure in the tests is a literal I worked out from the SARS tables by hand, so the test is an independent check rather than a snapshot of the code."

Part 05The craft concepts

05The craft concepts

C1

The conceit: the site is a workbook

What it is
Rather than a scrolling page with sections, the site is a spreadsheet: a formula bar, one open sheet, sheet tabs, a status bar, and a name box. Click any number and the formula bar shows how it was worked out.
Where it lives
portfolio/index.html and portfolio/assets/css/styles.css, section 27.
Why it matters
It is the thing that makes the site memorable, and it is not decoration — it is the interface for the work. A payroll person recognises a spreadsheet instantly, and the site's own subject matter explains its own design.

Say it like this: "I made the portfolio a workbook rather than a scrolling page, because that is the tool the work actually lives in, and it lets me show the formula behind every number."

C2

One authored moment

What it is
One deliberate transition — the sheet changing — and nothing else that moves without a reason. The mouse-following cursor glow and the 3D card tilt were both removed.
Where it lives
portfolio/assets/js/main.js and the sheetIn keyframe.
Why it matters
Scattered motion reads as decoration; one moment reads as a decision. The two effects that were cut were also the most expensive things on the page while scrolling.

Say it like this: "There is exactly one animation — the sheet change. Everything else that moved was decoration, and two of those were the biggest cost while scrolling."

C3

The frame budget

What it is
At 60fps a frame is 16.7ms; on a 120Hz screen it is 8ms. Only transform and opacity skip layout and paint, so those are the only things worth animating. A blurred background costs a great deal more than it looks like.
Where it lives
The performance work in styles.css — the removal of the blurred aurora, the flat surfaces, and the deliberate use of transform: scaleX() rather than animating a width on the progress bar.
Why it matters
It is the difference between a site that feels cheap and one that feels considered, and it is measurable rather than a matter of taste.

Say it like this: "I only animate transform and opacity, because those skip layout and paint, and I took out the blurred background — it looked like nothing and cost a great deal."

C4

Themed browser surfaces

What it is
The scrollbar, the text selection, the caret, the focus ring and the numerals in the tables are all themed from the palette. The figures use tabular numerals so the columns line up.
Where it lives
styles.css — the scrollbar and selection rules, and font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums on every money column.
Why it matters
These are the parts of a page nobody drew, and they are the first thing that gives away that a page was assembled rather than built. For a finance site the numerals matter doubly: a column of Rand amounts that does not line up is a broken ledger.

Say it like this: "I themed the scrollbar, the selection and the caret, and the money uses tabular figures so the columns line up — in a spreadsheet, ragged numbers are a bug."

The one thing that makes the tests worth having

Every expected value is a literal, typed in by hand from the SARS tables: 

# a R30 000 a month salary, 2027 tax year, 34 years old
#   annualised taxable   = 30 000 x 12                     = 355 200
#   slab tax             = 44 118 + 26% x (355 200 - 245 100) = 72 744
#   less primary rebate                                  = 17 820
#   less medical credit (R1 576 a month x 12)           = 18 912
#   annual tax           = 72 744 - 17 820 - 18 912    = 36 012
#   monthly PAYE         = 36 012 / 12                  = 3 001

close(calculatePayslip(thandi, "2027", 2_664_000).payeMonthly, 3_235)
close(calculatePayslip(thandi, "2027", 2_664_000).netMonthly, 27_087.88)
The distinction worth being able to state. A test that recomputes the expected answer with the same function it is testing proves nothing beyond "the function is deterministic". Every expected number here is a literal I calculated myself, so a change in the code that breaks the tax shows up as a failure instead of quietly agreeing with itself.

The tests that run in the browser

The JavaScript engine is a second, independent implementation of the same rules. It carries the same hand-worked cases, and you can run them yourself in the console: 

// open the browser console on the site and type:
Tax.selfTest()   // 12 checks — the PAYE engine
Vat.selfTest()   // 19 checks — VAT, SDL, payslips, the EMP201

This is the thing I would point at first in an interview. Two implementations, written from the same source tables, agreeing on every figure — that is a much stronger claim than "I wrote some tests".

What is deliberately not tested

  • No mocking of the tax rules. The whole point is that the real tables are used.
  • No test for a tax year that does not exist. There is a validation error, but no test for the behaviour of a table from 2024, because there is no table from 2024.
  • No end-to-end browser test in CI. The CI runs the Python suites on four versions. The browser self-tests run when you open the console, and I check them by hand before every release.
  • No load or performance testing. Six employees is not a scale test, and I would not claim otherwise.
Part 08Hosting

08How it is hosted

The website runs on GitHub Pages, free, forever, with no server to pay for and nothing to maintain.

The website — GitHub Pages

The site is pure static files, so it does not need a server at all. GitHub serves it straight from the repository, and a workflow republishes it whenever the portfolio folder changes. It costs nothing, there is nothing to patch, and it cannot go down.

I also added the workflow rather than using the built-in "deploy from a branch" option, because Pages can only publish the root of a branch or a /docs folder — and this site lives in a portfolio folder inside a repository that also holds the Python projects. A workflow can point anywhere, and it means the site is only rebuilt when something in it actually changed.

The payroll app — PythonAnywhere

The payroll suite needs a process that stays running and a database, so it cannot be static. It is set up to run on PythonAnywhere's free tier through the WSGI adapter.

The honest limitation: the free tier has one app, a subdomain rather than a custom domain, and on some hosts the filesystem is ephemeral — so the database would need to live somewhere that persists. For a portfolio I would rather run it from my own machine when I am demoing it than pretend it is a hosted product.

One thing I would say about the deployment if asked. The site is deliberately static and dependency-free, which is not a limitation I worked around — it is the reason it still builds in five years with nobody maintaining it. The CI is the part I would defend hardest: it is what makes "push and it works" a safe thing to say.
Part 09The mistakes

09The mistakes I made

This is the most useful part of the document. Anyone can list what they built. Being able to say what went wrong, and what the fix was, is what separates a person who has built something from a person who has only read about it.

1. The annualised method, done wrong

The first version divided the month's pay by the number of months the employee had worked. That produced a tax figure that halved every month for somebody who joined in November — because "months worked" was growing while the pay stayed the same. The numbers looked plausible and were completely wrong.

The fix was to go back to the SARS guide and read how annualisation actually works, then rebuild the model around monthly taxable × 12. The mid-year joiner problem is real, but it belongs to the year-end reconciliation, not to the monthly calculation.

What I would say: "My first annualised implementation was wrong — I divided the month's pay by the months worked, which halved the tax each month for a new joiner. I caught it because the test fixtures stopped agreeing with the hand-worked numbers, and the fix was to read the SARS guide properly rather than patch the arithmetic."

2. A formula that did not add up

The formula bar shows the working for every number. To check it, I wrote a small evaluator in Python, rebuilt the formulas the JavaScript builds, and compared what the formula evaluates to against what the cell displays.

It found three bugs that no screenshot would have shown:

  • the net pay formula omitted the medical contribution, so it was R1 200 out;
  • the slab formula was hardcoded to the second band, so it was wrong for anyone earning more than R383 100;
  • the rebate was hardcoded to the primary rebate, so it was wrong for a pensioner.

All three were invisible on the page, because the page was showing the engine's answer, which was correct. Only the explanation was wrong — and the explanation is the part a reader trusts.

What I would say: "I wrote a spreadsheet evaluator to check that every formula in the formula bar actually evaluates to the number next to it. It found three wrong formulas — a missing medical contribution, a hardcoded tax band, and a hardcoded rebate that ignored the age rebates. The numbers on the page were right; the story about them was not."

3. A class name that meant two things

The income-breakdown bar and the toolbar at the top of the page were both called .bar. The toolbar's rule came later in the stylesheet, so the breakdown bar inherited a 54-pixel height and the wrong background. It rendered as an empty grey pill.

The fix was to rename the breakdown to .breakdown — and to notice that I only found it because I took a screenshot and looked at it. The DOM said the segment was 80 pixels wide with the right colour. It was, and it was still invisible.

What I would say: "Two different components were both called '.bar', and the cascade gave the one underneath the other's styles. I found it by taking a screenshot and noticing the bar was empty while the DOM said the segments were eighty pixels wide. I renamed it, and I now give the two of them different names on purpose."

4. Reading the wrong keys

The payroll demo in the browser read payslip.gross_remuneration — the name the Python model uses. The JavaScript engine returns grossRemuneration. Every row in the table read "undefined".

The fix was to make the two engines use the same names, and then to look at the rendered page rather than the code.

5. Four tests that were lying

Four checks in the browser self-test were comparing a string with a numeric helper, so Math.abs("01 Mar 2026" - "01 Mar 2026") was NaN and the comparison was always false. They had never passed; they had never been run in a browser.

The fix was a comparison helper that knows about both, and then running the browser tests as part of the check before every release.

6. A page that would not lay out

I rewrote the stylesheet with a script that deleted rules by regular expression. One pattern ate a closing brace, and the page came out with the sheet area collapsed to zero height and the content invisible.

The fix was to stop patching and write the stylesheet properly, with a script that parses the file into rules, changes whole rules, and refuses to write the file if the braces do not balance. It is a small thing, but it is the reason the design pass is trustworthy.

The pattern behind all six: every one of them was a case of trusting that the code was right because it looked right. The fixes were all the same shape — make the computer check the claim. A test that evaluates the formula. A screenshot that looks at the pixels. A brace counter that refuses to write a broken file. A test in the browser rather than only in Python.
Part 10Questions, with answers

10Questions you might be asked

Twenty-two questions, grouped by who is likely to ask them. The answers are written to be said out loud — short, specific, and with a number in them. Read them once, then close the document and try from memory.

About the accounting

How do you calculate an employee's PAYE?Accounting
I annualise the month's taxable income, tax it on the SARS slab rates, subtract the age rebates and the medical tax credit, and divide by twelve. For a R30 000 salary that is R355 200 annualised, R44 118 plus 26% of the amount over R245 100, less the R17 820 primary rebate, less the medical credit, which gives R36 012 a year and R3 001 a month.
Why does the tax get annualised?Accounting
Because the brackets are built around a year of income, so you cannot tax a month on its own. Annualising also smooths things: a December bonus does not push somebody into a higher band for the whole year, and a month with no overtime does not drop them into a lower one.
What is the section 11F limit and why does it exist?Accounting
A pension deduction is capped at the lowest of the actual contribution, 27.5% of remuneration, and R350 000 a year. It exists to stop high earners turning a pension fund into a tax-avoidance scheme — without it, most of a large salary could be sheltered.
A 68-year-old earns R95 000 a month. What do they pay?Accounting
Let me work it: R95 000 a month is R1 140 000 annualised, less the R4 750 pension, so R1 083 000 taxable. That is in the band over R887 000, so R259 783 plus 41%, which is R340 143. She gets the primary and secondary rebate because she is over 65 — R27 585 — and the medical credit. That brings it to R24 171 a month.
What is the difference between a rebate and an exemption?Accounting
A rebate comes off the tax after the calculation; an exemption comes off the income before it. That is worth R17 820 a year to almost everybody, and the interest exemption works the other way — the first R23 800 is exempt, and only what is above it is taxed.
What is the medical tax credit, and what is the trap?Accounting
It is a credit against the tax for being on a medical scheme: R376 a month for the member, R376 for the first dependant, R254 for each one after, in 2027. The trap is that you keep the higher of the tabulated amount and what you actually pay, and it is a credit against the tax, not a deduction from income.
When would an employer not pay SDL?Accounting
Below R500 000 of annual payroll. It is 1% of the leviable amount — remuneration after the allowable deductions — and it is the employer's own cost, so it may not be taken off the employee's pay.

About the employer's compliance

What are the three forms an employer files?Compliance
The EMP201 is monthly — the total payment split between PAYE, UIF and SDL, due by the 7th of the following month. The IRP5 is per employee per year — their tax certificate. The EMP501 is the annual reconciliation that proves the twelve EMP201s add up to what the IRP5s say was paid.
Why does your code roll deadlines back a day?Compliance
Because a deadline that falls on a weekend or a public holiday is not on that day — it moves to the last business day before. An October 2026 payroll is due on Friday 6 November, because the 7th is a Saturday. There is a test for exactly that case.
How does the VAT201 period work?Compliance
Six two-month periods in a tax year — March–April, May–June, and so on through to January–February — and each return is due in the month after the period ends. So the March–April period is due in May. Businesses over R30 million of taxable supplies file monthly instead.
What is the year-end reconciliation you built?Compliance
Because annualising taxes somebody as if they would earn a full year, a joiner is over-taxed during the year. The reconciliation works out their real liability on what they actually earned, compares it with what was deducted, and gives the difference back on the last payslip. In the demo, an employee who joined in November was taxed R4 180 he did not owe, and the system gives it back.

About the engineering

Why no dependencies?Engineering
It means the project runs on any machine with Python, with no install step and nothing to go stale in two years. It also keeps me honest — I had to understand the tool rather than the wrapper around it. The trade-off is that I built the small things myself: the argument parser, the HTTP server and the CSV writer.
How is the code structured?Engineering
Three layers that only point one way. The domain layer is the SARS rates and the shapes. The application layer is the engine, the compliance rules and the storage. The presentation layer is the CLI and the API. The engine never imports the database, which is why 58 of the 148 checks test the tax on its own.
Why is there a database at all?Engineering
Because a payroll without history is a calculator. The database holds the employees, the pay runs and the payslips, and it lets the EMP501 reconcile a whole year. It is one SQLite file, so backup is copying it and the whole business fits in an email attachment.
What does the API look like?Engineering
Eight JSON endpoints over a small WSGI application — an overview for the dashboard, the employees, the payslips for a month, the EMP201, the deadlines, the IRP5 certificates, the EMP501 reconciliation, and one endpoint that runs a payroll. No framework: the standard library's http.server plus about 380 lines of my own.
How do you know the tax is right?Engineering
Two ways. First, every expected figure in the test suite is a literal I calculated by hand from the SARS tables, so the tests are an independent check. Second, the tax engine exists twice — once in Python and once in JavaScript — written from the same tables, and both carry the same test cases. If the two implementations disagree, one of them is wrong, and the tests find out which.
What would you do differently?Engineering
I would write the browser tests into the CI instead of running them by hand before a release. And I would keep the tax tables in one file that both engines read, rather than maintaining the same numbers in Python and JavaScript — right now a change has to be made twice, and a test is what catches the time somebody forgets.

About the design

Why does the site look like a spreadsheet?Design
Because that is the tool the work actually lives in, and it lets me show the working. Click any number in the PAYE model and the formula bar shows the actual accounting — the slab, the rebate, the medical credit — rather than a line of code. It is a portfolio that demonstrates the portfolio.
What is the one thing you would change about the design?Design
The palette started as the usual cyan and violet on near-black, and it looked like every other portfolio. Replacing it with warm ink and brass, and taking out the frosted glass, was the change that made it feel like a document instead of a dashboard. I would do that earlier.
How do you know the site is fast?Design
Two ways. I only animate transform and opacity, because those skip layout and paint, and I removed the blurred background and the frosted glass, which were the expensive parts. And the page measures its own frame rate while you scroll — if the machine cannot keep up, it turns the decoration off and says so.
What is the performance mode for?Design
The same reason a spreadsheet has a degraded mode. The site looks the same and does exactly the same work; it just stops pretending to be a document with a glowing mouse cursor on it. I would rather degrade honestly than have the site stutter on a four-year-old laptop.

About how you work

How do you make sure you have the right rules?Process
I go to the source. The SARS rates page, the Budget tax guide, the guide for employers in respect of employees' tax. Then I put the figures in one file with the source in a comment, so there is exactly one place to change when the rules do. In 2026 the VAT registration threshold went from R1 million to R2.3 million on 1 April, and that came from the Budget guide, not from memory.
Tell me about a bug you found.Process
The annualised one, which I described in Part 9 — my first implementation halved the tax each month for a new joiner. What I would emphasise is the method: the hand-worked test figures disagreed with the code, so I went back to the SARS guide rather than adjusting the code until the test passed. That is the difference between fixing a symptom and fixing a rule.
What is next?Process
Provisional tax. The IRP6 estimator for a freelancer with no employer to deduct PAYE, the first and second payments, and the comparison against the final liability. It is the one part of the employee's side of the tax I have not built, and it is the part that most people who use the tax tables never need.
